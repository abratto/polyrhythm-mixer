Welcome to Alan's Polyrhythm Mixer
Layer two competing meters into a groove — tap in patterns, add voices, and shape the sound.
Pick a polyrhythm. Choose Meter A and Meter B (try 3 against 4), set a tempo, and press Enable Audio. A default pulse is pre-tapped so you hear the groove right away.
Tap steps and add voices. Click boxes in any lane to toggle hits. + Voice layers extra rhythms in the Master, Meter A Phrase, or Meter B Phrase lanes. Nudge shifts one voice; Nudge Group shifts the whole lane.
Extend with phrase lengths. Set A, B, or Master Phrase Length to 2–8 cycles for longer repeating patterns. When a lane has multiple cycles, use Cycle ◀ / ▶ to browse — the label shows AUTO (follows the playhead) or PIN (manual view).
Mix and share. In the Sound Mixer, swap instruments, solo, mute, and adjust volume per channel. Save stores rhythms locally; Share copies a link; Reset Mixer restores defaults.

Polyrhythm Visualization

Gears. The centre wheel is one measure of music: it turns once per 4 beats, and every tooth is one pulse. With 6 against 4 it has 12 teeth — Meter A fires every 2, Meter B every 3. The 4 grey spokes inside are the quarter-note beats. Pink marks show where Meter A's pulses land, cyan where Meter B's land; when both land on the same tooth the colour blends to magenta — both meters firing together. Use the Gears / Rings switch (above the picture) to flip between this mechanical view and the clock-face view.

Side wheels. The two small gears show how the ratio is constructed: they mesh with the centre wheel, so fewer teeth means more turns — Meter A's wheel (2 teeth) turns 6 times per measure, Meter B's (3 teeth) turns 4 times, once per meter pulse. A dot above each wheel also flashes whenever that wheel completes a turn; the centre wheel's flashes on every quarter note.

Rings. A clock-face view of one measure, shown on its own so it stays easy to read. The outermost ring is the full pulse grid (12 dots for 6 against 4); the pink and cyan rings carry Meter A's and Meter B's numbered pulses; the innermost orange ring numbers the four quarter beats. A hand sweeps around once per measure — as it passes each mark, that pulse fires, and where it lines up marks from two rings at once, both meters hit together. Switch back to Gears to see the mechanical construction of the same ratio.

Align. Three lanes share one measure laid out left to right: the 4/4 beat on top, then Meter A and Meter B. Vertical connectors mark every tick where two meters fire together, and the counter below states how many pulses remain until the next alignment — the moment an ensemble player counts down to.

Voice. The polyrhythm spoken aloud, after the Anlo Ewe hand-rhythm tradition (C. K. Ladzekpo). The measure becomes a spoken line: Kpla on every coincidence — both meters striking together, both hands; Ka on each lone Meter A pulse — the strong hand; Tu on each lone Meter B pulse — the weak hand. The big word is the syllable to say right now; the line below shows the full spoken sequence for the measure. Say the words in time and your hands tap out the polyrhythm.

Shapes. Each meter's pulses joined into a closed polygon on its ring — a hexagon for 6, a square for 4, star shapes for odd meters. Stacked in one dial, the polygons show the structural geometry of the ratio at a glance.

Master Cycle Timeline. The same single measure, laid out left to right: dots = Master voice hits, ◆ = Meter A/B pulse positions, ▲/▼ = phrase-lane hits, white line = playhead.

Full Pattern Timeline. Every cycle of the whole pattern chained end to end, so you can see the complete repeating loop and how the meters realign across cycles — especially useful with longer phrase lengths.
